Setup

Do the two judges agree?

Rank correlation and agreement over 200 answers.

The Gemma-4-31B judge is more lenient than the Qwen3.5-27B judge across the board, including for Tiny Aya, so the gap looks like general leniency rather than a preference for its own family. Neither judge favoured the Qwen candidates.

Are the differences real?

Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on per-question overall scores.

Caveats

  1. Reference answers were machine-translated from expert English answers. Judges were told to grade facts, not wording.
  2. Judges are large models, not agronomists. A blind sheet of all 200 answers is ready for human rating and should be the final word.
  3. CPU timing used a Colab Xeon, not a phone. Measure again on the target device before the field trial.
  4. Each model answered once (seed 42). Rankings rest on 50 questions.

Recommendation

Prototype with Gemma 4 E2B. Fix its knowledge before the field.

Use Gemma for the first demo

It is the best model on every quality and fluency measure, and the lead over Tiny Aya is statistically significant. The notebook's automatic pick, Qwen3.5 2B, fits the RAM budget but scores 1.04 / 5 on quality and 1.98 on fluency, which is not usable for a demo.

Ground the answers

Even Gemma gets only 18% of answers mostly right and gives harmful advice in 21 of 50. Retrieval from the expert-validated knowledge base, then fine-tuning on Urdu agronomy Q&A, is the next lever, not a bigger model.

Shrink the footprint

Gemma peaks at 4.3 GB of RAM. A smaller context window, lower-bit quantisation or a distilled student should bring it under 3 GB. Then confirm human ratings and phone timings.
